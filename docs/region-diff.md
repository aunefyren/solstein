# Region diff module

`modules/regiondiff` removes dynamically inserted ads by downloading each episode through two exits in different ad markets and keeping only the audio both share. Nothing is re-encoded: the output is the home download's own frames, byte for byte. Where the host splices ads in as whole frames (Acast, Dovetail), the two downloads are compared frame by frame; where it re-encodes the episode around its ads (RedCircle), they share no frame, and are compared by their loudness over time instead, read from the compressed spectrum without decoding to sound (below). It is an episode processor ([`architecture.md`](architecture.md)); the frame reader it uses is the `mp3` package, the loudness measurement `mp3/spectrum`.

## Why it works: how Acast inserts ads

Measured on one episode of a show with Norwegian dynamic ads ("Corner Piece"), downloaded twice from Norway (direct) and twice through a Swedish Proton exit (2026-09-25):
- Same region, same moment: byte-identical. Different regions: different (42,848,705 vs 43,044,093 bytes; the feed states 38,273,358).
- Both files are CBR 128 kbit/s, 44.1 kHz MPEG-1 Layer III: one 188-byte ID3v2 tag and nothing but frames, ending in a cut-off frame (200 of 417 bytes in the Norwegian download, 399 of 418 in the Swedish one). No Xing/LAME header, no junk.
- **Acast splices whole MP3 frames without re-encoding.** 92,128 frames (40.1 min, against the feed's 39:52) are byte-identical in both, in the show segments; the ad breaks — a pre-roll, mid-rolls at about 15 and 31 minutes, a post-roll — are runs of frames that differ.
- **Every splice point starts on a clean frame** (`main_data_begin = 0`: it borrows nothing from earlier frames through MP3's bit reservoir). Acast encodes each segment on its own, so cutting there leaves no glitch. Only 34 and 29 frames in the whole files start clean: the segment starts.
- Short runs of identical frames also occur inside ad breaks (silence encodes identically, e.g. runs of 3 and 31 frames in the post-rolls). They don't start on a clean frame and are short, which tells them apart from show audio.
- Ads vary over time, not just by region: downloads hours later through a Proton Norway exit carried different ads from the direct Norwegian ones, but byte-identical show audio.

## The MP3 reader (`mp3`)

- Reads ID3v2 (several tags, footer), ID3v1, Xing/Info/VBRI info frames (kept apart from the audio: they describe the uncut file), and MPEG-1/2/2.5 Layer I–III frame headers including CRC and Layer III's `main_data_begin`.
- A header is only believed when another valid one follows it, at the start and after any skipped bytes; the stream's version, layer and sample rate must stay the same. Junk bytes (`Skipped`) and a cut-off last frame (`Truncated`) are counted, never fatal.
- No Solstein dependencies. Fuzzed (21 million inputs: no panic, every byte accounted for as tag, frame, skipped or truncated).

## The diff (`Diff`)

`Diff(home, other, options)` removes from `home` everything it doesn't share with `other`. (`NewComparison(home, options).With(other)` does the same, and keeps what it learned of the home download for the next comparison: the processor compares it with one fallback after another.)
1. Parse both; a file that isn't MPEG audio (e.g. AAC in `.m4a`) is `ErrUnsupported`. Two files in different formats (sample rate, MPEG version) can't share frames, and go straight to the comparison by audio.
2. **Hash every frame** and **align** (if no frame at all is shared, the host re-encodes: compare by audio instead): anchors are groups of 16 frames (~0.4 s) that occur once in each file; the largest in-order set of anchors is kept (longest increasing subsequence), and each is extended into the longest run of equal frames. With about 100,000 frames per episode this avoids a quadratic diff. Runs are verified byte for byte, so a hash collision can't let other audio in.
3. **Trim each run to real segment boundaries:** it must start on a clean frame (or the file's first frame) and end where the next frame starts clean (or the file ends); otherwise it is cut back to just before the last clean frame inside it. This handles breaks that end in identical silence (the run would start early) and that open with an identical jingle (it would end late). **Except** when the stretch before the run's first clean frame (or after its last) is at least 30 s: shared audio that long is show, not silence or a jingle, so it is kept from where the match starts (or up to where it ends). Some hosts (PRX's Dovetail) don't splice on clean frames, and the show resumes after an ad on a frame that borrows from the one before (below, under Write).
4. **Keep runs of at least `min_shared_seconds`** (default 2 s, about 77 frames). Everything else is ad.
5. **Sanity checks:** at least one run kept, at most `max_removed_share` (default 30%) of the home file removed, and the result no more than 5% shorter than the feed's `itunes:duration` when it states one; otherwise `ErrImplausible`. Longer than stated is accepted: the result can't be longer than the home download, so it only means ads the same in both regions are left in, which is still better than all of them. The episode's note then says so ("still 2m14s longer than stated: ads the same in every compared region may be left").
6. **Write:** the home download's ID3v2 tag, the kept frames copied one by one, its ID3v1 tag. No info frame (it would describe the uncut file) and no cut-off last frame.
   - **Silent frames at cuts:** a kept frame right after a cut may borrow up to 511 bytes from the removed frames before it (Layer III's bit reservoir). Cut plainly, it would decode against whatever bytes now precede it: a corrupted frame. So the fewest removed frames that hold those bytes are kept as **silent frames** (`mp3.File.SilentFrame`): the same header and size, the side information zeroed (no audio data of their own, so they play as silence; the CRC recomputed if there is one), and every other byte unchanged, so the borrowed bytes are exactly where the kept frame looks for them. A cut then costs a moment of silence, one or two frames (26 ms each at 44.1 kHz) at typical bitrates, instead of a glitch. Acast's cuts need none (its segments start on clean frames); a re-encoded file nearly always does. For Dovetail, where the show's frame borrowed from the ad already in the host's file, it now decodes exactly as it does there.
   - The result's duration includes them.

`ErrIdentical` means the audio is the same (same bytes, or nothing removed from either side).

With `TrimBreakMarkers`, break markers are removed between steps 4 and 5, so the sanity checks see the final result (below).

On the live pair the diff takes under a second and keeps the three show segments (13:22, 15:10, 11:34), removing the four breaks (1:31, 1:00, 0:40, 1:21): 40:06 against the feed's 39:52. Diffing in either direction gives byte-identical show audio, and ffmpeg decodes the result with no errors.

Shared ads stay in: audio that is the same in both regions is kept as show audio, including an ad running in both markets. Host-read ads baked into the recording can't be found by any diff.

## Hosts that re-encode: comparing by audio

Found on "Safety Third" (RedCircle, `audio4.redcircle.com`, 2026-09-25): 14 episodes failed in production, as "the downloads differ in format" (48 kHz against 44.1 kHz) or "share no show audio". The kept downloads showed why:
- **Norway got the producer's original upload:** 192–320 kbit/s, 44.1 or 48 kHz depending on the episode, with Adobe Media Encoder's tags, playing within 1 s of `itunes:duration` every time.
- **Germany or Sweden got a copy re-encoded as a whole around the ads:** always 128 kbit/s, 44.1 kHz (Lavf), 20 s to 2½ min longer. Decoded, the show audio is the original's throughout, shifted by the ads: in episode 49, a 30.1 s pre-roll; in episode 160, a 75.2 s mid-roll at 42 min and 59 s at the end.
- So not a single frame is shared, and a frame diff can never work between the original and a stitched copy, or between two stitched copies. 55 other episodes were "no dynamic ads found": every market got the original.

**Measuring loudness (`mp3/spectrum`):** each frame's spectrum is Huffman-decoded and requantised, and its energy summed per granule (576 samples); both channels together, stereo processing skipped (mid/side coding keeps the total). Turning the spectrum back into sound is skipped: that was 71% of a full decode. The decoding is a trimmed copy of `github.com/hajimehoshi/go-mp3` (Apache-2.0; see `mp3/spectrum/README.md`), with a table of quarter powers in place of `math.Pow`. An 86-minute episode takes about 7 s, against 44 s for go-mp3's full decode; the two downloads are measured at the same time. A frame that can't be decoded counts as silent; with more than 5% of them the file is refused. It never panics on bad data (recovered, and fuzzed: 1.5 million inputs). Memory: the copy reuses its per-frame structures, so comparing episode 160's two downloads (232 MB) allocates about 1 GB over its 6.7 s and peaks at 570 MB resident, against 423 MB for just reading and parsing them; allocating per frame as go-mp3 does took 5.9 GB, 646 MB and 9.2 s. Measuring stops when the pipeline's context is done (shutdown). The loudness is then resampled to one value per 10 ms, as log energy with a floor about 90 dB down, so files with different sample rates line up in time.

**Lining up (`audioDiff`):**
1. **Find runs:** the home download's loudness, averaged to 100 ms, is cut into 5 s windows every 2.5 s, and each is looked for in the other's by normalised correlation (so level differences between encoders don't matter). A window is taken at the offset of the one before when it correlates at 0.9 or more there; otherwise the whole range of plausible offsets is searched, and the best match counts if it is at least 0.85 and beats the best match anywhere else (more than a window away) by 0.1. Windows much flatter than the episode's typical one (silence, steady noise) are left out: they would match anywhere.
2. Consecutive windows at one offset form a **run**; runs under 10 s are dropped, and of the rest, the heaviest set in order in both downloads is kept (so a false match out of order goes), with neighbours at one offset merged back where a stray window split them.
3. **Refine:** each run's offset to 10 ms, from up to a minute of its middle; then each is followed out to where its audio stops matching (0.3 s of it averaging over four times the run's typical difference, plus a margin), so a cut falls at the break rather than at a window's edge.
4. **Cut:** what of the home download no run covers, if it is 5 s or more, is removed, at its own frame boundaries (a frame goes with the side its middle is on), and written with silent frames at each cut (above). What of the other download no run covers, 5 s or more, is counted as its ads (`OtherExtra`, `OtherBreaks`). Why 5 s: around each place RedCircle inserts an ad, a second or two of its copy differs from the original (1.3 s at a mid-roll, 2.3 s where a post-roll starts), and that is show audio in the home download; ads are longer. Two runs overlapping by more than a second in the other download mean the downloads don't line up: implausible.
5. **Outcomes:** nothing removed from either side is `ErrIdentical` (the same show, encoded twice); nothing removed from the home download, but ads in the other, keeps the home download whole, byte for byte, noted e.g. "no ads in the norway download: sweden's has 2 breaks (2m14s) more; compared by audio, as the host re-encodes". This is the evidence that the home copy is clean: every stretch of it is found, in order, in the other, and only the other has more. Otherwise the removed stretches are cut, noted "… by audio". The usual sanity checks apply.
6. **Bounded:** the full searches are capped at 10¹⁰ multiply-adds (about 10 s); past that the comparison fails as implausible ("too hard to line up"), and the pipeline's retries and failure policy take over. The hardest real episode checked used 17% of it (1,084 full searches, 1.1 s), most a few percent. It also stops when the pipeline's context is done.
7. When the loudness can't be measured, the frame comparison's verdict stands: no frames shared is implausible (retried, as a fresh download may be better), different formats are `ErrUnsupported`.

Checked on the 15 kept Safety Third failures (2026-09-26): all 15 keep the Norwegian download whole, nothing removed, each within 1 s of the stated duration; the 18 kept Darknet Diaries sets still take the frame path with the same cuts as before. ## Break markers

Hosts splice a short chime or sting in around ad breaks. It is the same in every region, so the diff keeps it as show audio. "Corner Piece" has a 2.27-second chime (87 frames) six times per episode — at the start, twice back to back at each mid-roll position (before and after the break), and at the end — byte-identical in every episode of the feed (2026-09-25).

`trim_break_markers` (off by default) removes the ones that can be cut cleanly. A piece is taken for a marker only when all of these hold, so show audio is never cut:
- it is at the edge of a kept run, next to removed audio or the file's start or end;
- it is at most 5 s long;
- its frames are byte-identical to another such piece in the same episode;
- **and either** it is a whole spliced segment — it starts on a clean frame (or the file's first) and the frame after it starts clean (or the file ends), so cutting it leaves no decode error on either side — **or** it doesn't, but a piece the same bytes confirm elsewhere in the episode *does*, so its length and position are known without needing a clean frame of its own.

A marker encoded together with the start of a show segment (no clean frame after it) is trimmed too, matched by content against a clean occurrence of the same marker elsewhere: the show's first frame still borrows bit-reservoir bytes from it, so as many of the marker's last frames as that takes are kept as silent frames rather than dropped (the same mechanism as a cut at a break, `write`). Nothing is trimmed on a guess: a marker that is *never* spliced in cleanly anywhere in the episode has nothing to confirm it, and stays untouched. In "Corner Piece" that is the chime after each mid-roll: on the live pair (2026-09-25, before this), trimming removed only the four then-cleanly-cut chimes — at the start, before each mid-roll, at the end, the exact 87-frame pieces at frames 3471–3558, 34070–34157, 71199–71286 and 99325–99412 — leaving one chime per break instead of two, 40:06 becoming 39:57, ffmpeg decoding the result with no errors. The two glued chimes after each mid-roll would now also go, each down to one or two silent carrier frames; this has synthetic test coverage (`modules/regiondiff/diff_test.go`) but hasn't been re-checked against a live download since.

Off by default, and staying off by decision (2026-09-27): a show's own sting, if it is spliced in as its own segment at breaks, is structurally indistinguishable from a host's marker — same byte-identical-and-at-an-open-edge shape — and would be trimmed too. One show run with it live so far ("Corner Piece") never showed a false trim, but one show isn't enough to rule it out on others; the episode's note names every marker trimmed and its length ("…, including 4 break markers (9s)"), which is the audit trail if one ever looks wrong. Considered and not changed: defaulting it on, relying on that note as the safety net rather than the trim itself being provably safe.

## The processor

`regiondiff.Processor`, for each episode of a feed it handles:
1. **Check the countries.** Two exits in the same country get the same ads, so a diff between them would find nothing and the episode would be published with its ads. Before downloading, the processor asks which country each exit's next connection goes out in (`outbound.Manager.ExitCountry`). An exit that is in the home exit's country right now — say a loose exit that fell back — is skipped, and the first fallback in another country takes its place; with none left, the attempt fails and is retried later, when the exits may have moved. Exits whose country is unknown (servers without a location, and `direct` unless `home_country` is set) are trusted.
2. **One attempt at a time when the exits are short of tunnels.** The processor asks whether the pair and its fallbacks can all have a tunnel at once (`outbound.Budgeter`, which the exits module answers from each provider's limit). When they can't, attempts take turns: two at once would close and reopen each other's tunnels, which moves VPN keys between servers and can leave a tunnel stalling for minutes ([`exits.md`](exits.md)). The start-up log says so, and how many tunnels are missing. With room for every exit, attempts run as concurrently as the pipeline's workers allow.
3. **Download through both exits** — at the same moment, or one after the other with `pair_downloads` in turn (Settings) — with the same User-Agent, so the only difference is the region. If the partner's download fails, the fallback exits take its place in turn while the home download carries on (a host that stops answering through one exit doesn't fail the attempt); if the home download fails, the attempt fails. After a failed attempt, downloads ask caches on the way not to answer from a stored copy (`Cache-Control: no-cache`), in case the host's CDN served a broken one.
4. **Check each download.** A download that plays under 80% of the feed's stated duration (or, without one, of the other download), or isn't MP3 when the other is, looks like a cut-off or wrong file: it is downloaded again, fresh, once. If that is no better, the first is kept and the diff decides. This is checked because a host that serves a partial file with a matching `Content-Length` gets past the download checks, and would otherwise make the diff look implausible.
5. **Diff:** frame by frame, or by audio when they share no frame. The home exit's download is the one kept.
6. **Identical audio** means no dynamic ads, or the same campaign in both markets; two files can't tell which. Each of `fallback_exits` is then tried in turn against the home download (skipping any that is part of the feed's own pair). A fallback that can't be downloaded through is skipped with a warning rather than failing the attempt: the pair already agrees, so it can only add certainty, and failing would repeat every download on the retry (seen live when a Proton exit's resolver couldn't resolve NRK's CDN host). **So is a slow one:** a fallback download may take the home download's size at 1 MB/s, and at least 30 s (`fallbackTimeout`: 92 s for a 92 MB episode), and is then given up on, noted "(not compared through us: the download was too slow)". Why: in production (2026-09-28), with the pair `norway`/`germany` and fallback `us`, every NRK episode's pair agreed in about 17 s and then waited 15 minutes on US-AZ#108 → NRK (86 MB at about 95 KB/s): never idle long enough for the pipeline's 2-minute idle timeout, so nothing was logged, ABS got `503` twice per episode and moved on, and its single queue ([`clients.md`](clients.md)) held every later episode behind it. Reproduced on `docker-test` (9 minutes, ~160 KB/s, while `DE#187` delivered the same file at full speed in the pair, so not NRK throttling everything outside Norway); with the limit, the same kind of episode took 1 m 48 s in all. 1 MB/s is a judgement, not a measurement: a healthy tunnel does 5–9 MB/s ([`exits.md`](exits.md)), so a fallback under a fifth of that isn't worth the wait. Only the identical-audio fallbacks are bounded; a fallback standing in for a partner that failed is the comparison itself and runs to the pipeline's own limits. If every region reached agrees, the episode is kept as it is and noted "no dynamic ads found", with the fallbacks that couldn't be reached named in the note — with a hint when the file is more than 5% longer than stated, which suggests ads that are the same everywhere.
7. **Errors:** not MP3, or in different formats and not measurable by audio, fail for good (`ErrPermanent`); an implausible result, a failed download or a failed fallback is retried with the pipeline's back-off, since the next downloads may carry other ads. The error names both downloads' sizes and durations, e.g. "(norway: 105.2 MB, 1h54m16s; sweden: 52.1 MB, 57m2s)", so a bad download can be told from a bad diff. With `keep_failed_downloads`, both downloads and a note of the error are kept in `regiondiff-failures/{feedID}/{episodeID}/` in the config directory (replaced by the episode's next failure, removed after 14 days). With `keep_successful_downloads`, every diff that worked keeps its two downloads too, in `regiondiff-successes/{feedID}/{episodeID}/` with a `result.txt` giving the note and each removed segment's position and length in the home download (break markers labelled), so a bad cut that passed the sanity checks can be looked into. Identical downloads are kept as well, noted without cuts. Same retention; it costs twice each episode's size.
8. The result goes to the pipeline with its duration and a note, e.g. "removed 4m15s of ads in 4 breaks, comparing norway with sweden" ([`episodes.md`](episodes.md)).

## Batching the background queue

Short of tunnels, every attempt still moves the key twice — once to the home exit, once to the partner — however few episodes are running at once. For the pipeline's background queue only (never an episode a client is waiting on), `ProcessBatch` amortises that: it fetches every job's home download first, moves the key once, then fetches every job's partner and diffs each as `Process` would (`finishPair`). `BatchSize` offers a batch — 3 episodes, `batchSize` — exactly when `outbound.Budgeter` says the pair can't have a tunnel each, the same condition that already serialises attempts one at a time; with a tunnel each, episodes are still prepared individually and concurrently, as before.

The pipeline claims the rest of a batch (`database.Store.ClaimQueuedEpisodesForFeed`) only after claiming one queued episode normally, and only for that one's feed; an episode a client's own request claims in the meantime is left out of the batch and comes due again on its own schedule, the same as the equivalent race for a lone claim ([`episodes.md`](episodes.md), Background queue). Each job's outcome — cached, failed, retried, withheld — is recorded exactly as a single attempt's would be.

Bounded to 3 episodes and kept in memory (each still capped at `region_diff.max_episode_mb`, `episodes.Job.Fetch`): a larger, disk-spooled batch is a further idea, not built, and only worth it once this one needs it (`docs/wip.md`).

**Verified live (2026-09-27, one-key `docker-test`):** 15 "Safety Third" episodes queued at once via `/prepare` ran as 5 batches of 3, each logging one "Preparing 3 episodes… together" and one key move each way — not one pair per episode. 13 of 15 cached on the first pass; 2 failed in their own job only (an HTTP/2 connection lost mid-resume, a host `500`), without touching their batch-mates, confirming the per-job isolation live and not just in the synthetic tests. Re-queuing just those 2 through `/prepare` batched them together too (`BatchSize` doesn't require a full batch, just more than one waiting) and both cached clean on the retry — 15 of 15 in the end.

**Batched against unbatched, the same 15 episodes (2026-09-27, one-key `docker-test`):** episodes 122–136 of "Safety Third" (a fresh, never-downloaded backlog block, picked with `POST /prepare {"newest": 45}` so only they qualified) were processed twice, back to back, on the same one-key build: once queued together (5 batches of 3, as above) and once reset to uncached and fetched one at a time, each request blocking on its own `Prepare` (the on-request path, which `BatchProcessor` never touches). Batched: **366 s wall clock, ~24.4 s/episode**, 10 tunnel switches total (one pair per batch: 5 to `mine`, 5 to `abroad`). Unbatched: **~445 s of processing summed over the 15** (one request failed after 27 s with `http2: client connection lost`, the same class of mid-body reset already tracked above; a manual retry cached it in 20 s, in line with a client's own retry), **~30 s/episode average, 30 tunnel switches** (a pair per episode). Batching came out **about a fifth faster overall**, not from any parallelism — `ProcessBatch` fetches no more concurrently than `Process` does — but from needing a third as many key moves (10 against 30): each switch reopens a tunnel and, in this run, very often paid a DNS lookup through the fresh tunnel timing out once before a fallback resolver answered (`exits.md`, Tunnels). This confirms what `docs/wip.md` suspected rather than measured: batching's real saving is fewer stall-prone transitions, not raw throughput, and it is real but modest at this batch size and episode count.

Every episode of a processed feed is served cleaned: new episodes are processed before they are published, backlog episodes (and processed files past their retention) are processed on first request with a bounded wait, the newest `backlog` episodes are processed as soon as a feed is added, and any others can be processed ahead through the API ([`episodes.md`](episodes.md), [`feeds.md`](feeds.md)). The unprocessed version is served only when processing fails for good and the policy is `publish`; with `hide`, the episode is left out and tried again slowly in the background for about a week ([`episodes.md`](episodes.md), Background queue), and start-up logs a warning saying so.

## Settings

```jsonc
"region_diff": {
  "enabled": false,                  // default for feeds that don't set region_diff
  "exits": ["norway", "sweden"],     // the pair, home region first
  "fallback_exits": ["germany"],     // tried when the pair's downloads are identical
  "min_shared_seconds": 2,
  "max_removed_share": 0.3,
  "on_failure": "publish",           // publish (with ads) | hide
  "backlog": 0,                      // newest existing episodes processed when a feed is added
  "pair_downloads": "auto",          // auto | together (a tunnel each) | in_turn (one tunnel)
  "trim_break_markers": false,       // also remove break markers that can be cut cleanly
  "compare_by_audio": true,          // compare downloads a host re-encodes by their loudness
  "keep_failed_downloads": false,    // keep both downloads of a failed diff, for 14 days
  "keep_successful_downloads": false, // keep both downloads of every diff that worked, with where it cut
  "max_episode_mb": 512              // cap on each of the processor's own downloads, held in memory whole
}
```

- `pair_downloads`: `together` downloads the pair at the same moment, so the only difference between the two files is the region and an episode is cleaned in seconds; `in_turn` downloads them one after the other, so only one tunnel is open at a time and **one VPN key is enough**. In turn costs about twice the time per episode, plus any settling a moved VPN key needs ([`exits.md`](exits.md)), so it belongs with `prepare_ahead` ([`episodes.md`](episodes.md)): on request such an episode would pass the client's 20 s wait. `auto` (the default) asks the exits module whether the pair can have a tunnel each (`outbound.Budgeter`) and downloads in turn only when it can't; the start-up summary says which it chose, and a warning says what in turn costs. The diff doesn't mind the gap between the downloads: it keeps what they share, and a show's own audio doesn't change between them (below, Verified live). `pair_downloads` is global, not per feed: it follows the keys, not the show.
- `compare_by_audio` is a pointer in `settings.RegionDiff`, so a `config.json` without it gets `true` written in; off, downloads that share no frame fail as before (different formats `ErrUnsupported`, none shared implausible), and the recipe says "not by audio".
- In `config.json` only, like the VPN block. `settings.RegionDiff` checks the numbers (`min_shared_seconds` 0.5–60, `max_removed_share` above 0 and at most 1, `backlog` not negative, `max_episode_mb` 1–2048) and `on_failure`; a bad value stops start-up like any bad setting.
- `max_episode_mb` (default 512) caps each download the processor makes for itself — home, partner, each fallback tried, each episode in a batch (Batching, below) — held in memory whole (`episodes.Job.Fetch`; `episodes/pipeline.go`'s own plain-download path is separately capped at 2 GB, since it streams straight to the cache file rather than holding the whole thing in memory). Raising it costs peak memory: comparing by audio has measured at roughly 2–2.5× one pair's combined size (`docs/wip.md`, Comparing by audio). A show whose episodes are longer or at a higher bitrate than "a three-hour episode at 128 kbit/s" (Linus Tech Tips' WAN Show, found live 2026-09-27: several episodes past 460 MB, one over the old fixed 512 MB failing permanently) needs it raised, or those episodes are withheld for good, retried forever, never succeeding.
- **The pair:** `direct` works as the home side but shows the host the home address and needs only one tunnel; a VPN exit in the home country (e.g. Proton `NO`) gives the same home-region ads without that, over two tunnels, which one Proton key handles. With the direct exit off (`direct_exit`, off by default once VPN exits exist), it has to be a VPN exit.
- **Per feed:** `region_diff` (`on`, `off`, or empty for `enabled`), `region_diff_exits`, `region_diff_on_failure`, `region_diff_trim_break_markers` and `region_diff_compare_by_audio` (`on`, `off`, or empty for the global setting) ([`feeds.md`](feeds.md)). With `enabled: false` and `exits` set, region diff runs only for feeds that switch it on.
- **Start-up** (`regiondiff.Setup`, given the exits that exist): without `exits`, region diff is off and silent. It stays off with a warning when `enabled` has no `exits`, when there aren't exactly two, when one is named twice, or when one doesn't exist (with a specific message for `direct` while the direct exit is off). Unusable fallback exits are dropped with a warning.
- **Same country at start-up:** from each exit's locations and servers, the exits module lists every country it may come out in (`ExitCountries`). If both exits of the pair can only come out in one country and it is the same, region diff stays off with a warning. If they can overlap through fallback locations, it runs with a warning, and the check before each download (above) handles it. A fallback that can only come out in the home exit's country is dropped. `direct`'s country is unknown to Solstein (finding it would take an outside geolocation service), so it is never flagged — unless the operator declares it with the top-level `home_country` (e.g. `NO`), which `outbound.Manager` then reports as `direct`'s country (not while `direct` is disabled). A pair like `["direct", "norway"]` from Norway is then refused at start-up, and skipped before each download like any other same-country exit.
- Region diff never stops start-up. The log states the pair, fallbacks, default, failure policy and whether markers are trimmed.
- **Changing settings:** each cleaned episode records the settings it was cut with (`Processor.Recipe`: the pair, fallbacks, `min_shared_seconds`, `max_removed_share`, `trim_break_markers`, `compare_by_audio` and an algorithm version; version 4 compares by audio and keeps silent frames at cuts, so every episode is cut again once, and episodes that failed under version 3 are retried). When they change, the old file is cleared and the episode cut again on its next request ([`episodes.md`](episodes.md)). The failure policy isn't part of it: it doesn't change a cleaned file.

**A one-key setup, verified live through ABS (2026-09-27):** the [`docker-test`](../docker-test/README.md) harness, Solstein built from the checkout, one Proton key (`private_keys: ["env:PROTON_KEY_1"]`, the others in `.env` unreferenced), exits `mine` (`NO`) / `abroad` (`DE`), no fallback exits, `pair_downloads: auto` (resolved to in turn, as it must with one key), `prepare_ahead` off and both sides' timeouts raised to match (`processing_wait_seconds: 600`, ABS `PODCAST_DOWNLOAD_TIMEOUT: 720000`), against 15 backlog episodes of RedCircle's "Safety Third" (62–220 MB).

Every attempt failed at first, always the same way: `tunnel limit reached` switching the pool's one tunnel from `mine` to `abroad` (or, on the next episode, back from `abroad` to `mine`), after waiting out the full `tunnelWait` (a minute). The tunnel that had just finished a download was never idle by the pool's own accounting (`activeCount`, [`exits.md`](exits.md)): the exit's HTTP client keeps its connection to the podcast host pooled for reuse for up to 90 s (Go's default `IdleConnTimeout`), which outlasts `tunnelWait`, so the single tunnel could never free up in time — deterministically, every episode, both directions. **Fix:** `episodes.Job` gained `CloseIdle(exit)` (wired to `outbound.Manager.Client(exit).CloseIdleConnections()`, `episodes/pipeline.go`); region diff's `fetchPairInTurn` calls it on the home exit right after fetching it, before the partner (`modules/regiondiff/processor.go`), and `Process` defers a call on the exit it finishes on (the partner, or the last fallback tried), so the *next* episode's home fetch isn't blocked either.

With the fix, all 15 processed: tunnel handoffs that had deterministically failed after a minute now happened in the same second, every time, both directions. Each episode's full turnaround — both downloads in turn, the diff, the cache write — took 14–70 s depending on size, comfortably inside the raised timeouts; no client ever saw a `503`. One episode needed the pipeline's own retry after a transient Proton DNS timeout on `audio4.redcircle.com` (`docs/wip.md`, unrelated to the above), succeeding on its second attempt 35 s later. Most of this batch came back "no dynamic ads found" (Norway and Germany getting the same audio, as seen before with this host); one ("Is a Horse Girl or a Linux Guy More Insufferable") had 3m0s of ads removed across 3 breaks. 13 of the 15 were confirmed end to end, GUIDs kept, in ABS's library; the other 2 were cleaned and cached by Solstein correctly (also "no dynamic ads found") but weren't re-fetched by ABS, an artifact of restarting the Solstein container mid-test to deploy the fix rather than anything wrong with the fix itself.
